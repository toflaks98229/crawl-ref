Three Cells Wide

Crawl already draws some creatures bigger than the square they stand on: the standard tile is 32×32, but uniques get 32×48 (rltiles/dc-mon.txt:6) and nothing about the rules changes. A 3×3 delver works the same way — the anchor cell is still the one the game reasons about. What changes is that the cell is 1 wide and 2 tall, so three cells across is six tall: these figures are drawn at the proportion they will actually have.

Consequences

  • Keep the anchor one cell. Occupancy, line of sight, reach and cleave all read a single coordinate. Make the sprite a rendering concern and get_cleave_targets keeps working unchanged; make it nine real cells and every rule needs rewriting.
  • The walk cycle is the energy bar. One step costs 10 auts, so drive the cycle phase from the fill toward 80 rather than from wall time. The figure then finishes its stride exactly as the step lands, at any clock rate.
  • A one-cell corridor cannot hold a three-cell body. Either the level generator works at 3× scale — corridors three wide, which triples the map to roughly 200×70 cells — or the big sprite is reserved for open ground and the figure drops to one glyph in tight spaces, the way zoom already swaps detail in the battle game.
  • Scale is a statement about rank. Crawl gives the big tiles to uniques only. If everything is 3×3 the size stops meaning anything, and 683 monsters would each need a design.
