Looking Straight Down

From above, a person is round: shoulders across are about as wide as the body is deep. The cell is 1 wide and 2 tall, so a round footprint needs more columns than rows — the 3×3 block of the side-view study is a standing shape, and used overhead it reads as a body lying down. The other half of the problem is that the figure now has to point somewhere, and Crawl has no facing at all: neither player.h nor monster.h carries one.

What it costs

Consequences

  • Facing is state the rules do not have. Searching player.h, actor.h and monster.h for a facing finds nothing; a monster carries a target coordinate, not an orientation. Derive the sprite's heading from the last move — delve.html already keeps you.lastDir — and never let it feed back into a roll. The moment facing changes a to-hit, it is a different game.
  • You cannot rotate ASCII on this grid. Turning a 5×3 block a quarter turn gives 3×5, which in pixels is 45×150 instead of 75×90 — a different creature, not the same one facing east. So north and east are drawn separately, and the glyphs turn too: / becomes \, | becomes -.
  • Overhead hides the walk. From above the legs are under the body, so the stride has to be told with the arms and with a foot showing past the shoulder line. Two poses per facing carry it; the side view had four to spend.
  • The cost is per facing. Four cardinals at two poses is already 120 characters a design; adding diagonals doubles it, and every monster you scale up pays the same again.
